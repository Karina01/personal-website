<p>On a glorious August morning in Copenhagen, I dropped my daughter at <em>vuggestue</em> (nursery) and continued cycling across the soothing greenery of <em>Amager Fælled.</em> I was enjoying the fresh morning air and passing unfamiliar parts of my city, while building up an appetite for Den Bette’s BMO. Den Bette was recommended by a reader, which felt like a milestone for this little newsletter!</p><p>I almost biked straight past the front of it and then discovered that it was closed.</p><p>Disappointing, of course, but I also quietly admired their uncompromising opening hours. I decided to embrace the notion that Wednesdays <em>should </em>be a day of leisure by hopping on my bike for a few minutes to the beach, where I sourced a back-up BMO and spent two hours lying on the sand listening to my book.</p><p>My next attempt to visit Den Bette was notably more successful, in that it was open and serving BMOs. Tucked on a residential corner near splashes of street art, Den Bette lives up to its name, which means ‘the little one’ in Jutland dialect. It’s tiny, perhaps the size of a living room, and has previously been, among other things, a vinyl records shop, a storage room for residents and a bike shop. In its current bakery form, it has an undeniably cosy feel, with one full wall of freshly baked goods.</p><figure class="post__figure"><img src="/images/writing/den-bette-the-little-one-before-the/01.webp" alt width="1456" height="945" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure><p>It’s a family-owned micro-bakery, started by a married couple in November 2021 who were seeking a change of scene after Covid. As I ordered our BMOs, I saw the baker in the back room jabbing a large loaf into the oven and sensed a justified pride in their  morning’s efforts from those behind the counter. This café seemed like a go-to spot for locals: two frazzled mums one-arm-hugged outside while jiggling their strollers, a young woman tapped away on her laptop, a fashionably dishevelled man sipped a coffee with relief.</p><figure class="post__figure"><img src="/images/writing/den-bette-the-little-one-before-the/02.webp" alt width="1456" height="1151" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure><h2>Onto the BMO</h2><p>I ordered the <em>Ølandsbolle</em> which is a sourdough bread bun made with an old Nordic wheat variety, sourced from an organic micro-farm (Grønsund) based on Møn. My husband chose a sourdough walnut and cranberry bun.</p><p>You can select two toppings on your bun, one of which is the butter. You then have the option of a medium-aged cheese or Thise’s <em>Vesterhavsost</em>, for an additional charge. We chose the <em>Vesterhavsost</em>, which is a firm cheese with a slightly nutty flavour. We also ordered a small pot of raspberry jam on the side for a sweet treat, plus two Americanos. The coffee beans are from Copenhagen Coffee Lab - ours were served piping hot and had a fresh, fruity note.</p><figure class="post__figure"><img src="/images/writing/den-bette-the-little-one-before-the/03.webp" alt width="1456" height="996" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure><p>You could tell that both buns were baked that morning, with a good chew and crisp outer edge. The <em>Ølandsbolle</em> was round and springy with small pockets for the butter. The walnut and cranberry bun was a little more dense, so the butter formed a smooth layer on top. There was quite a lot of flour on the outside of both buns - so much so that I was blowing flour off my phone screen as I typed up my review notes. The taste and texture it left in my mouth were a little more than I’d choose, but it did add to the just-out-of-the-oven experience.</p><figure class="post__figure"><img src="/images/writing/den-bette-the-little-one-before-the/04.webp" alt width="1456" height="953" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure><h2>Where to sit</h2><p>There’s very limited seating inside, including a couple of window seats and a few cushion-clad chairs. A hand-written sign outside even requests that guests only queue two at a time inside, to respect the experience of those who manage to nab a seat.</p><figure class="post__figure"><img src="/images/writing/den-bette-the-little-one-before-the/05.webp" alt width="1456" height="948" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure><p>Outside are wooden chairs and tables with burlap-sack seats, fleece blankets and parasols to help navigate the changeable Copenhagen weather. Walking a little further along the street are some public outdoor benches framed by a canopy of winding leaves. You can also pop into the old graveyard nearby (<em>Sundbyøster Kirkegård)</em> to sit on a bench by fading gravestones and towering trees.</p><figure class="post__figure"><img src="/images/writing/den-bette-the-little-one-before-the/06.webp" alt width="1456" height="765" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure><h2>The verdict</h2><p>Den Bette is a lovely place to enjoy a freshly-baked BMO while the quiet rhythms of the day unfold. There are a variety of tasty buns to choose from, and perhaps give them a gentle tap on the plate to remove some flour! Given the limited indoor seating, I’d visit Den Bette on a sunny day and, unlike me, check the opening hours first: enjoy a BMO outside, pack some pastries and continue on to a day at the beach.</p><p>(PS.
